Remembering Longtime Sports Broadcaster Dan Hicks and His Career

The search trend "Dan Hicks wife" is surging because ESPN broadcaster Hannah Storm publicly announced the passing of her husband, longtime NBC Sports broadcaster Dan Hicks, at the age of 64 following an acute illness. Storm shared a statement on X noting that her family is heartbroken by the sudden and unexpected passing of her husband of over 30 years and loving father to their three daughters, stating that his loss is immeasurable.

Dan Hicks was a prominent and respected voice in sports broadcasting. He joined NBC Sports in 1992 and spent 34 years with the network as an exceptional storyteller and caller of games and events. He served as NBC Sports' lead play-by-play announcer on PGA Tour coverage starting in 2000, anchoring golf coverage for major events including the U.S. Open, The Open Championship, Ryder Cup, Presidents Cup, and the U.S. Women's Open.

In addition to his extensive golf broadcasting career, Hicks covered 16 Olympic Games—15 as a play-by-play announcer—beginning with the 1992 Barcelona Games. Most recently, he worked as the Alpine skiing play-by-play announcer for NBC Sports at the Milan Cortina Games. He also served as the lead play-by-play announcer for NBC Sports' coverage of Notre Dame football for a combined seven seasons, which included his return to the role in 2024 alongside previous years covering the Fighting Irish from 2013 to 2016. Earlier in his career, Hicks worked as a sports anchor for CNN starting in 1989. His decades in the industry earned him widespread recognition, including a Sports Emmy for his work on the 2024 Paris Games.

Following the news of his passing, tributes poured in from colleagues, sports executives, and athletes across the sporting world. ESPN chairman Jimmy Pitaro extended condolences on behalf of the ESPN family to Hannah Storm, their three daughters, and Hicks' NBC colleagues. Fifteen-time major golf winner Tiger Woods and NBC Sports executives Rick Cordella, Sam Flood, and Molly Solomon also shared statements praising his professionalism, kindness, warmth, and lasting impact on the sports world. NBC paid tribute to Hicks before its broadcast of Notre Dame's home matchup against Boston College, where colleagues Ahmed Fareed, Jason Benetti, Jason Garrett, and Jimmy Roberts remembered his career, warmth, and humor.

Remembering Dan Hicks: Colleagues and Tributes Honor the Legacy of a Broadcasting Icon

The sudden passing of Dan Hicks left the sports broadcasting community grappling with profound grief, as colleagues and friends shared their memories of a man who was as respected for his character as he was for his talent. During the broadcast of Notre Dame’s home matchup against Boston College, NBC dedicated time to honor his memory, with studio host Ahmed Fareed opening the coverage by noting that the team was still in shock. Fareed spoke with visible emotion about celebrating a man whose expertise, warmth, and humor left a permanent mark on everyone he encountered, emphasizing that the day was dedicated to remembering and missing a dear friend.

Other members of the NBC broadcast team shared similar sentiments regarding the void left by his absence. Jason Benetti remarked that the broadcast booth felt hollow, expressing that he wished he did not have to be there and that Hicks should have been the one calling the game. Jason Garrett, who partnered with Hicks on Notre Dame broadcasts beginning in 2024, highlighted his selfless approach to the job. Garrett recalled that Hicks constantly looked out for him and frequently asked if he could "tee you up," noting that Hicks never made the broadcasts about himself. Instead, Hicks focused entirely on embracing the moment and highlighting the individuals involved, a philosophy that reportedly defined how he lived his everyday life.

Jimmy Roberts, who collaborated with Hicks for years on NBC’s golf coverage, introduced a comprehensive video tribute highlighting the remarkable breadth of his career. Roberts reflected on the magnitude of the loss by observing that the depth of sorrow typically mirrors the depth of love, though the reality of Hicks not being in South Bend to do what he loved remained difficult to comprehend.

Beyond the broadcast booth, tributes emphasized the personal warmth that accompanied his professional achievements. Colleagues frequently described him as a genuinely better person off the air than even his legendary status suggested—someone who consistently brightened every room he entered and welcomed teammates with a smile, a joke, or a comforting word of encouragement. Tiger Woods also shared high praise for the broadcaster, highlighting the kindness and presence that will be deeply missed both on and off the golf course. Woods noted that Hicks had been a great friend to him for decades, commanding the utmost respect for the exceptional work he contributed to the sport. The widespread outpouring of remembrance from executives, athletes, and network partners underscored the profound, enduring influence Dan Hicks maintained across the entire sports world throughout his illustrious career.

Why This Matters and What to Watch Next

The sudden loss of a broadcasting fixture like Dan Hicks serves as a profound reminder of the tight-knit bonds within the sports media industry and the heavy human cost behind major public events. When figures who have spent decades guiding audiences through legendary moments—spanning Olympic Games, golf's most prestigious majors, and collegiate football—are suddenly taken, it profoundly shifts the atmosphere for their colleagues and fans alike. The raw emotion displayed on air by broadcast teams at NBC, alongside statements from figures like Tiger Woods and network executives, highlights how deeply professional admiration can intertwine with personal affection. It demonstrates that the lasting legacy of a prominent sports voice is measured not only by the historic calls they preserve on tape, but by the daily acts of mentorship, generosity, and camaraderie they offer behind the scenes to partners and crew members.

As the sports world continues to process this sudden transition, attention naturally turns toward how networks like NBC will honor his towering contributions moving forward and how his longtime colleagues will navigate the physical absence of a mentor in the booth. Viewers and sports organizations will likely keep watching for permanent tributes, memorial scholarships, or legacy programming dedicated to his 34-year career. Meanwhile, the outpouring of support for ESPN broadcaster Hannah Storm and their three daughters underscores the enduring human element of public life, leaving family, friends, and fans to honor a devoted husband and father whose presence shaped decades of televised sports.
