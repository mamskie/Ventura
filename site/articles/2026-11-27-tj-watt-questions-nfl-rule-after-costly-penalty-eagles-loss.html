T.J. Watt Questions NFL's Low-Hit Rule After Costly Penalty in Steelers' Loss to Eagles

On Sunday afternoon at Lincoln Financial Field, the Pittsburgh Steelers lost 19-16 to the Philadelphia Eagles in a game decided by three points — and by a single flag. In the fourth quarter, with 7:19 remaining, star linebacker T.J. Watt was called for roughing the passer after making low contact with Eagles quarterback Joe Milton. The penalty gave Philadelphia a first down, extended a drive that ended in a field goal, and left Watt questioning a rule he says he does not know how to follow.

The call came on first-and-10 at the Philadelphia 35. Milton's pass to DeVonta Smith fell incomplete, but the flag gave the Eagles a fresh set of downs at the Pittsburgh 45. Milton found Darius Cooper for 14 yards, and Jake Elliott drilled a 55-yard field goal to stretch the lead to 19-9 with under five minutes to play. The Steelers scored late — Aaron Rodgers finding Max Hurleman for a touchdown with 1:26 left — but could not close the gap, falling to 6-4 while the Eagles ended a losing run that stretched back to Week 2.

What happened on the play

The rule at the centre of the argument is the NFL's ban on low hits on the quarterback, a restriction meant to protect passers from knee and leg injuries. Watt was flagged for making contact below the waist with Milton as he closed in for the tackle. Watt's case, made in his postgame press conference, was that the contact was not his choice: an offensive player pulled him down as he tried to make the play.

"It's tough when you're getting pulled down, you know, it's hard in those situations," Watt said. "Felt like there was one early in the game too with the tight end where you get involved and they pull you down to get out, so it's one of those things where I'm not sure how you avoid it. I'll have to look at the film."

Watt said the incident was not a one-off. He pointed to an earlier play in which a tight end pulled him down in a similar way, and argued that he regularly finds himself in that position when he tries to pressure opposing quarterbacks. His point was sharpened by what happened on another play: Philadelphia's right tackle was called for holding after pulling Watt to the ground — the same kind of contact, penalised on the offence rather than the defence.

The flag was Pittsburgh's third 15-yard penalty of the game, and it capped a costly afternoon of discipline problems. The Steelers were penalised six times for 49 yards, against four penalties for Philadelphia. Much of the damage came from wide receiver DK Metcalf, who was flagged twice for unsportsmanlike conduct — the second after a taunting penalty and grabbing cornerback Cooper DeJean's face mask — and ejected from the game. It was the third disqualification of Metcalf's NFL career since he was drafted in the second round in 2019, and he left having caught one pass for two yards from four targets.

A game the Eagles controlled late

The penalty arrived in a game Philadelphia largely controlled in the second half. Milton, making only the second start of his career, guided the Eagles to their first win since Week 2, improving them to 3-7. He sealed the result with a seven-yard out route to DeVonta Smith — back for the first time since the Week 6 game against Carolina — on third down in the closing minutes, trusting a receiver he praised for the way he shows up to work.

Elliott kicked four field goals, and Saquon Barkley's 38-yard touchdown run — in which he escaped Watt in the backfield before breaking down the left sideline — gave Philadelphia a lead it never gave back. "It feels good to break a long run," Barkley said. "As a back, that helps your confidence. That helps the confidence of the offensive line."

Philadelphia's defence, meanwhile, made life miserable for Aaron Rodgers. The Eagles sacked the veteran quarterback five times, with Byron Young recording two and Gabe Hall — elevated from the practice squad — collecting the first sack of his NFL career. With All-Pro linebacker Zack Baun on injured reserve, Jeremiah Trotter Jr. led the game with 13 tackles and Jihaad Campbell added seven. Pittsburgh's ground game, one of the league's best over the first ten weeks, managed just 73 yards; Rodgers, with 29, was the Steelers' leading rusher.

Why Watt's complaint will linger

Watt's frustration is not just about one flag in one game. It goes to a tension the NFL has wrestled with for years: how to protect quarterbacks without making it impossible for defenders to do their jobs. The low-hit rule exists because a defender diving at a passer's legs can end a season, and the league has tightened it over the years. But Watt's argument is that the rule, as enforced, leaves no room for the reality of a pass rush — a defender being dragged down by a blocker has little control over where his body ends up.

His suggested fix borrows from another part of the rulebook. On punts, a player who is blocked into the punter is not penalised for roughing or running into the kicker, because the contact was forced on him. Watt's case is that the same logic should apply when a defender is pulled into a low hit on the quarterback. It is a narrow, specific proposal — not a call to scrap the rule, but to recognise when the defender had no choice — and it is the kind of argument that tends to reach the league's competition committee in the offseason, when plays like this one are reviewed.

For the Steelers, the timing stings. At 6-4, they remain in the thick of the playoff race, but the loss to a 3-7 Eagles team is the kind of result that narrows the margin for error, and the discipline problems — six penalties, an ejection, three 15-yard flags — are the sort of self-inflicted damage that coaches spend the week trying to fix. Watt now has a fresh grievance with the rulebook to carry into that week — and a film session in which, by his own admission, he will be looking for an answer he is not sure exists.

The Eagles, meanwhile, will take the win. They snapped a losing streak that had lasted since Week 2, their young quarterback won his second career start, and a defence patched together without its All-Pro linebacker held one of the league's better rushing attacks to 73 yards. The flag on Watt will be debated; the result will stand.
