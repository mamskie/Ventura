DK Metcalf Will Not Be Suspended After Ejection, Clearing Him for Black Friday

Pittsburgh Steelers wide receiver DK Metcalf will not be suspended by the NFL after his ejection from Sunday's 19-16 loss to the Philadelphia Eagles, ESPN and NFL Network reported on Monday, clearing the way for him to play when the Steelers host the Denver Broncos on Black Friday. The league will still review his actions for fines as part of its standard disciplinary process, but the decision ends several days of uncertainty over whether the league would sideline one of its most penalised players at a moment when Pittsburgh can least afford to lose him.

Metcalf was thrown out in the second half at Lincoln Financial Field after picking up his second unsportsmanlike conduct penalty of the afternoon, an automatic disqualification under league rules. The flashpoint came when he appeared to try to poke Eagles defensive back Cooper DeJean in the eye — an act DeJean said afterwards "just missed" him. The official game book described the first of the two fouls as unnecessary roughness, but the ejection itself was automatic once the second flag fell.

What happened in Philadelphia

The ejection capped a miserable afternoon for Metcalf on the field. When he was tossed, he had 30 penalty yards and just two receiving yards — 15 times as many yards in flags as in catches. The Steelers went on to lose 19-16, their second consecutive defeat since a 6-2 start had them looking like a comfortable playoff team at the trade deadline.

His coach and quarterback did not hide their frustration after the game. "We need him in those games," Steelers head coach Mike McCarthy said. "That's not acceptable to get thrown out a game." Quarterback Aaron Rodgers added: "I mean that was disappointing. Obviously, we love DK, and I don't know what was going on out there, what was happening, but yeah, we can't have two of those [penalties]." Metcalf himself denied on Monday morning that he had tried to poke DeJean, saying he "can't remember" the interaction with the Eagles defender.

The search for answers — and for the league's ruling — kept the story alive all week. The phrase "dk metcalf suspension" surged on Google Trends in the United States on Friday, the day of the Broncos game, as fans checked whether the receiver would be available for the Black Friday game. The ruling was first reported on Monday, three days before kickoff, and the wait for it kept Metcalf's name near the top of American searches all week.

A history the league could not ignore

The league office had more than Sunday to weigh. Metcalf was suspended for the final two games of the 2025 regular season after an in-game altercation with a fan in Detroit, and NBC Sports reported that history could be taken into account when the league decided on further discipline. That was not his only brush with the league over eye-poking: in November 2025 he was flagged for unsportsmanlike conduct and fined $12,172 for fighting after trying to poke Green Bay Packers linebacker Quay Walker in the eye.

The numbers behind the flags are stark. Since entering the league in 2019, Metcalf has drawn 16 "conduct" penalties — unsportsmanlike conduct, unnecessary roughness, taunting or disqualification — five more than any other player in that span, according to TruMedia. Over his career he has been called for 42 accepted penalties, 11 more than any other wide receiver, per NFL Research. This season alone he has drawn eight accepted penalties, tied for fourth among players at all positions and twice as many as any other receiver.

McCarthy, speaking on Monday, said he had held a "productive" conversation with Metcalf. "Life's about learning situations, and I do believe in second chances," he said. "I will judge him based on my personal interactions with him." Metcalf, for his part, deflected when asked about his struggles keeping his composure: "I think you got to ask the people that seemingly do that to me," he said.

Why the decision matters for Pittsburgh

The ruling keeps Metcalf on the field for a game Pittsburgh badly needs to win. The Broncos visit Pittsburgh on Black Friday, and a loss — combined with a Houston win — would let the Texans pass the Steelers in the standings if Houston then beats Pittsburgh in Week 13. The Steelers are watching a once-comfortable position slip, and a franchise facing a 10th straight season without a playoff win if it falls short again.

The irony is that Metcalf has offered little when he has stayed on the field. Through 10 games he has 36 catches for 434 yards and two touchdowns for the NFL's 23rd-ranked passing offence, and his 12.3 yards per reception, 6.2 yards per target and 40.0 per cent success rate would all be career lows if the season ended today. Last season brought career lows of 59 catches and 850 yards, and a dropped pass in the playoff loss to Houston became the emblem of a miserable first year in Pittsburgh.

That record sits awkwardly against the price Pittsburgh paid: a second-round pick to Seattle and a four-year, $132 million contract. He carries a $1.3 million base salary for 2026 with three seasons left on the deal, no guaranteed money remaining and a $38.4 million cap number in 2027. If his struggles continue, the Steelers could move on with a post-June 1 cut or trade next year, absorbing $11.925 million in dead money while saving $26.5 million against the cap. For now, though, the league's decision means the argument stays on the field — starting tonight, against Denver, with Pittsburgh's season suddenly looking fragile.
