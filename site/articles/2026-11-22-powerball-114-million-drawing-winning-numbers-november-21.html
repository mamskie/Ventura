Powerball's $114 Million Drawing: The Winning Numbers for November 21

The numbers are in, and the wait begins. Saturday night's Powerball drawing carried an estimated $114 million jackpot, and the winning numbers — 5, 29, 33, 36, 45, with the Powerball 22 — are now public. What is not yet known is whether anyone matched all six of them.

The drawing, held on the evening of November 21, followed a Wednesday draw that produced no grand-prize winner, which pushed the jackpot to $114 million with a $47.9 million cash option. It was enough to send “powerball winner” to the top of search trends across the United States overnight, as players checked their tickets and the country waited for lottery officials to confirm whether the jackpot had been won.

What happened

The winning numbers for Saturday, November 21, were 5, 29, 33, 36 and 45, with the red Powerball 22. The Power Play multiplier drawn was 2X, doubling any non-jackpot prize won with that add-on. As of the latest update, lottery officials had not yet confirmed whether a ticket matched all six numbers to take the $114 million prize — or whether the jackpot would roll over again.

If no ticket claims the prize, the jackpot keeps growing ahead of the next drawing on Monday, November 23, at about 10:59 p.m. Eastern time. Powerball drawings are held three times a week — every Monday, Wednesday and Saturday — and can be watched live on Powerball's website, with replays on its YouTube channel.

The jackpot had been building since November 4, when a New Jersey ticket won an estimated $847 million — the ninth-largest prize in the game's history. Since then, a string of drawings without a jackpot winner has steadily pushed the prize upward, from $92 million on Wednesday, November 18, to Saturday's $114 million.

A deeper look: a jackpot that keeps climbing, after a record year

Saturday's $114 million prize is modest by the standards of a game that has produced some of the largest jackpots in lottery history. The record remains the $2.040 billion won in California on November 7, 2022. Behind it sit $1.817 billion in Arkansas on December 24, 2025; $1.787 billion split between Missouri and Texas on September 6, 2025; $1.765 billion in California on October 11, 2023; and $1.586 billion shared across California, Florida and Tennessee on January 13, 2016.

The rest of the top ten tells the same story of prizes that grew for weeks before falling: $1.326 billion in Oregon on April 6, 2024; $1.080 billion in California on July 19, 2023; $1 billion in Illinois on August 12, 2026; $847 million in New Jersey on November 4, 2026; and $842.4 million in Michigan on January 1, 2024. Against that backdrop, $114 million is a mid-size jackpot — big enough to dominate a Saturday night, but still early in a run that could grow much larger if it keeps rolling over.

It has already been a remarkable year for Powerball winners. The 2026 jackpot wins so far include $209.3 million in North Carolina on January 21, $250.8 million in Arkansas on March 2, $230.8 million in Delaware on April 6, $143.4 million shared by tickets in Indiana and Kansas on April 30, $20 million split between Florida and Texas on May 2, the $1.04 billion Illinois win on August 12, and the $847 million New Jersey win on November 4.

The mechanics are straightforward, and they explain why jackpots roll over so often. A ticket costs $2 per play and is sold in 45 states, Washington, D.C., Puerto Rico and the U.S. Virgin Islands. Players pick five white-ball numbers from 1 to 69 and one red Powerball from 1 to 26, or let the computer choose with a Quick Pick. For an extra $1, Power Play can multiply non-jackpot prizes by 2X, 3X, 4X, 5X or 10X. To win the jackpot, a ticket must match all five white balls and the red Powerball — odds long enough that most drawings end without a grand-prize winner.

Smaller prizes keep most players in the game: matching just the Powerball alone is worth $4, and a ticket matching one white ball and the Powerball is also worth $4. Matching two numbers wins nothing unless one of them is the Powerball. Players can also add Double Play for another $1, a second drawing with a chance at $10 million.

Powerball is not the only game drawing crowds this weekend. Friday night's Mega Millions numbers were 32, 35, 48, 58, 61 with Mega Ball 21, and that jackpot stands at an estimated $645 million — with a $270.8 million cash option — for Tuesday night's drawing, November 24.

Why it matters

First, the jackpot's fate sets the tone for the week ahead. If a winner is confirmed, Monday's drawing starts over at a reset jackpot; if not, the prize climbs again, and the cycle of rollovers that built the game's billion-dollar prizes begins again. Either way, the next drawing is Monday night, and the numbers will be watched just as closely.

Second, the surge in searches shows how a lottery drawing becomes a shared national moment. “Powerball winner” led US searches not because of a single ticket, but because millions of people held the same numbers at the same time and wanted the same answer. The draw is a ritual now — a Monday, Wednesday and Saturday appointment that briefly turns a numbers game into a conversation.

Third, the timing matters for anyone holding a ticket. Prizes come with a limited window to claim them, and unclaimed tickets are a recurring story in the lottery world. Anyone holding a ticket from Saturday's drawing should check the numbers — 5, 29, 33, 36, 45 and Powerball 22 — and check them soon.

For now, the $114 million question is unanswered. The numbers are public, the cash option stands at $47.9 million, and the next drawing is Monday at 10:59 p.m. Eastern. Until officials confirm a winner, every ticket from Saturday night is still, just possibly, the one.
