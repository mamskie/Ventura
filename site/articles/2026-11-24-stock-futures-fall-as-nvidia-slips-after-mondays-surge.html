Stock Futures Fall as Nvidia Slips 3% After Monday's 31% Surge

United States stock futures pointed lower on Tuesday as Nvidia slipped 3%, giving back part of the 31% surge that had powered Monday's sharp rebound in artificial intelligence stocks. The pullback spread across the major indexes, with the Nasdaq Composite down 1.8%, the S&P 500 down 1.2% and the Dow Jones Industrial Average down 0.6%, as traders used the retreat to reset expectations for the Federal Reserve's December meeting.

The slide came barely a day after one of the most violent reversals of the year. On Monday, Nvidia closed up more than 31% at $165.05, and the chip sector followed: Nasdaq 100 futures had gained 2.8% by 8:15 a.m. Eastern time, while S&P 500 futures rose 2.4% and Dow futures added 1.8%. Broadcom gained as much as 5.5% in premarket trading and Advanced Micro Devices rose more than 4%.

The whiplash has a clear trigger. Friday's sell-off — the Nasdaq fell 5.28%, the S&P 500 dropped 3.7% and the Dow lost $949.73 — followed a Reuters report that the Securities and Exchange Commission is investigating Nvidia's accounting practices. Nvidia fell about 14% that day. Monday's rebound followed President Donald Trump's weekend call for Nvidia's board to bring back Jensen Huang, and the board reinstated him as chief executive on Monday, days after saying it had no intention of doing so.

Tuesday's session is now testing whether Monday was the start of a broader reset or just one sharp session. Dell reports after Tuesday's closing bell, and a batch of economic data lands on Wednesday before the market shuts for Thanksgiving on Thursday — a thin week in which a single indicator or headline can move prices sharply.

What happened

Futures turned lower in early Tuesday trading as Nvidia gave back part of Monday's gain. The reversal hit the benchmark indexes in sequence: a strong artificial intelligence-led bounce on Monday, then a fast check on Tuesday over whether that move could hold.

Asia told a different story overnight. South Korea's Kospi rose 6% and Japan's Nikkei climbed nearly 4% — a sharp contrast with Monday, when the Kospi ended down 3.31% at 4,708.54. The divergence showed how uneven the rebound has been, even as the same handful of chip stocks drives the moves everywhere.

Friday's damage is the yardstick for everything since. The S&P 500 fell 3.7% on Friday to 6,910.39, leaving it more than 10% below its October 6 record — the threshold for a market correction — and down 8.6% for the week. The Dow ended Friday at 48,147.33, an 8% weekly decline, while the Nasdaq lost 10% for the week and finished at 22,617.73, down 2.7% for the year. The Russell 2000 fell 7.5% for the week, including a 2.8% decline on Friday.

A deeper look: rates, chips and a split earnings picture

The rate outlook moved with the market. Traders now price a 95% chance of a 25-basis-point increase by the Federal Reserve in December, with 5% assigned to a 50-basis-point move. Analyst Krishna Guha said the artificial intelligence sell-off was “not mostly due to the Fed,” but added that “it is not a coincidence it is occurring when the Fed is hiking.” He warned that “Fed Chair Warsh not talking is a problem,” leaving the market guessing whether the chair is “a hardcore hawk or a market-sensitive hawk.”

The debate over what the chips themselves are worth is running alongside the rate debate. Federal Reserve Governor Michael Barr said in a November 16 speech that the specialized chips inside data centers “may depreciate quickly as newer technologies overtake them,” though he added that rental rates for older chips had held up. Nvidia's own numbers point to ferocious demand: quarterly sales more than doubled from a year ago to $115.8 billion, its data center business accounted for $107.6 billion, and the company forecast about $130 billion in sales for the following quarter, excluding sales of data center chips to China.

Tuesday's earnings split the artificial intelligence trade in two. Hewlett Packard Enterprise fell 13% in premarket trading after fourth-quarter results, as the company said cloud and artificial intelligence operating margins are expected to fall to about 14% in fiscal 2027, down from 18.4% in the latest quarter — even as quarterly cloud and AI revenue grew 68.6% from a year earlier. Dell fell 7% ahead of its own results after Tuesday's close. Best Buy reported third-quarter earnings with same-store sales growth of 6.1% and revenue up 5% to $10.2 billion in Jason Bonfig's first quarter as chief executive.

The rotation continued elsewhere. Spot gold rose 2.4% to $3,617.68 a troy ounce, and Bitcoin traded above $61,000 after falling below $56,000 late last week. Anthropic rose about 4% in early trading on Monday, its third day as a public company after pricing its initial public offering at $83 a share. And CrowdStrike agreed on Monday to buy cybersecurity company Zscaler for about $43.9 billion in stock, a premium of about 34%, sending Zscaler up more than 17% in premarket trading while CrowdStrike fell more than 8%.

Why it matters

Three sessions have told three different stories about the same market: a crash on Friday, a rebound on Monday and a pullback on Tuesday. That is the signature of a market wound tightly around a single company and a single trade — when one report about an accounting investigation can erase trillions in value and one board decision can add much of it back in a day, the margin for error on every headline that follows is vanishingly small.

The Federal Reserve is part of that fragility now. A rally that feeds straight back into rate expectations — traders pricing a near-certain hike in December while still assigning a small probability to a half-point move — means the market's own strength can tighten the conditions it trades on. Guha's warning about a silent Fed chair captures the mood: with the chair not talking, others fill the gap, and their views can lag the leadership.

What comes next will be decided quickly. Dell's results after Tuesday's close are the next read on the artificial intelligence hardware trade, and Wednesday brings the PCE price index and revised third-quarter GDP before the market closes for Thanksgiving on Thursday. In a week with few trading days, a single data point can set the tone — and after three days of whiplash, investors are watching whether Tuesday's pullback was a pause in the rebound or the start of a deeper reversal.
