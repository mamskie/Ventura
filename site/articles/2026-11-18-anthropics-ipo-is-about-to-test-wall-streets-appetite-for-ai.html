Anthropic's IPO Is About to Test Wall Street's Appetite for AI

Anthropic, the artificial intelligence company behind the Claude chatbot, is about to find out whether Wall Street is still willing to pay top dollar for AI. The final price of its initial public offering is expected on Wednesday evening, with trading set to begin on Thursday on the Nasdaq under the ticker symbol “ANT.”

The offering is already shaping up to be one of the largest in stock market history — and possibly the largest ever. At the top of its projected price range of $70 to $78 a share, the deal would value Anthropic at up to $1.4 trillion. And strong demand from investors may push the price even higher: people familiar with the matter told The Wall Street Journal that the company could price its shares above $80, with advisers discussing a valuation of around $1.5 trillion or $1.6 trillion.

The listing arrives at an unsettled moment for the AI trade. Anthropic’s own hopes for its market value ran as high as $2 trillion as recently as September, and the slide since then reflects a market that has cooled on artificial intelligence. As of Monday, the tech-heavy Nasdaq had fallen about 5% over the past month. Yet the order book tells a different story: buyers have put in orders for shares at seven times the volume expected to be offered, according to a Bloomberg report cited by Investopedia.

What happened

Anthropic set out to sell its shares to the public at $70 to $78 apiece, a range that would raise around $61 billion before a separate private placement. Seven large technology companies — including chipmaker Nvidia, Alphabet’s Google and Microsoft — have agreed to buy another $26 billion of stock in that private sale, at the same price as the public offering. Together, the public offering and the private sale would total $90.4 billion at the top of the range, more than the record $75 billion that SpaceX raised in its June listing.

Up to 20% of the shares on offer are earmarked for individual investors, who can request shares through Charles Schwab, E*TRADE, Fidelity, Robinhood or SoFi. No one is guaranteed an allocation, and with demand running far ahead of supply, most requests are likely to be scaled back sharply.

The final price is not expected until Wednesday evening, and it will be set against a noisy backdrop. Anthropic has spent recent days pitching investors while Wall Street and Silicon Valley have been gripped by the dramatic exit of former Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang. The Journal reported that much depends on the market reaction to Nvidia’s earnings call on Tuesday, the first since Huang’s departure, which will be read as a health check on the whole AI trade.

A closer look at the demand

The demand is not just broad — it is unusually large. Altimeter founder Brad Gerstner said on CNBC on Monday that his firm had requested $5 billion worth of shares. Hedge funds and other large investors have placed larger orders than usual, according to people familiar with the matter, even after a punishing month of volatility in AI-linked stocks following the midterm elections. Investors in hot offerings typically submit orders above what they hope to receive, knowing they are unlikely to get their full request — but the scale here still stands out.

Anthropic’s own backers are leaning in rather than cashing out. Venture capital firms including Bessemer Venture Partners, Menlo Ventures and Accel plan to increase their investments in the IPO, the Journal reported. That is unusual: venture capitalists often exit long-held investments around the time of a listing. But Anthropic is not yet six years old, and many of its largest investors have held their stakes for two years or less, giving them more room to double down on a company growing as fast as this one.

There are reasons for caution, too. Heavy demand at the offering has not protected this year’s IPO buyers: shares of the 10 biggest US listings of 2026 have fallen about 20% on average from their offer prices, according to a Financial Times analysis. And the retail crowd has not clamoured for Anthropic shares quite the way it did for SpaceX in June — although some individual investors say they plan to buy in anyway, to avoid being left behind as AI sets the pace for the stock market.

Why it matters

If Anthropic prices at or above the top of its range, the listing will be a statement about the durability of the AI boom. A $90 billion-plus raise would make it the largest IPO ever, surpassing SpaceX’s record from June, and it would put a public price on a company whose Claude models have become a staple for businesses and coders. For the wider market, the debut will be read as a verdict on whether investors still believe the AI trade is worth paying a premium for — or whether the autumn sell-off was the start of a longer reassessment.

It also matters for the structure of the AI industry itself. The $26 billion private placement ties Anthropic’s biggest customers and suppliers — Nvidia, Google and Microsoft — even more closely to its fortunes, blurring the line between partners and shareholders. And with up to a fifth of the offering reserved for individuals, the listing is a rare chance for ordinary investors to buy into a leading AI company at the moment it goes public, rather than years later at a far higher price.

The answer comes quickly. The price is set on Wednesday evening, trading starts on Thursday, and by the end of the week the market will have delivered its verdict — in the price of ANT, in the size of the first-day move, and in what it means for the next AI company lining up to follow Anthropic onto the public markets.
