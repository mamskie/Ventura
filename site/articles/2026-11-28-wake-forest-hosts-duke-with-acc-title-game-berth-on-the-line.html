No. 8 Wake Forest Hosts Duke With an ACC Title Game Berth on the Line

Saturday afternoon in Winston-Salem is the biggest stage Wake Forest football has played on in years. The No. 8 Demon Deacons host Duke at 3:30 p.m. ET at Allegacy Federal Credit Union Stadium, with a place in the ACC championship game waiting at the end of it — and with ESPN’s College GameDay broadcasting from campus in the morning, the whole country will be watching a rivalry game that, a few months ago, almost nobody had circled.

Wake Forest is 10-1, 7-1 in the ACC, and one win away from locking in a championship-game berth. Its only defeat all season came against No. 12 Miami, 39-21. Duke arrives at 7-4, 4-4 in the conference, still stinging from a 28-24 rivalry loss to North Carolina on Friday — and cast firmly in the role of spoiler, with a chance to wreck the season of a team that has spent the fall surprising almost everyone.

What happened

The Demon Deacons’ rise has been the story of the ACC season. A week ago they dismantled Georgia Tech 42-3, their most emphatic win of the year, and the victory lifted them to No. 8 in the rankings and into the thick of the conference title race. In the league’s latest power rankings they sit fifth, behind Virginia, Miami, SMU and Georgia Tech — but with a clearer path to the championship game than any of them except Virginia.

That path runs straight through Saturday. With no tiebreakers needed, Miami and Wake Forest are set to face off for the ACC championship, provided Wake Forest takes care of business against Duke. A loss, and the picture gets complicated fast — which is exactly why Saturday carries the weight of a playoff game for the home side, even though it is technically just the regular-season finale.

The occasion matches the stakes. College GameDay is in Winston-Salem, and the show’s visit has dominated the week in town, from the university’s athletic director talking up the honour of hosting to Little Richard’s Barbecue serving as guest chef for the broadcast. The Demon Deacons are 11.5-point favourites, the over/under is 53.5, and the moneyline has Wake Forest at -450 against Duke at +340, according to odds from BetMGM. Fans lined up hours early with handmade signs, and the best of them were already making the rounds online before kickoff.

How the two teams match up

Wake Forest’s case rests on an offence that has been one of the most productive in the country. Quarterback Gio Lopez and running back Demond Claiborne lead a unit averaging 37.5 points a game, and the Demon Deacons have scored at least 30 points in each of their last four games. Lopez’s form earned him a place among Wake Forest’s Week 12 honours, and the 42-3 win over Georgia Tech was the latest reminder of how quickly this team can pull away from an opponent.

Duke, meanwhile, arrives with a new problem and a few reasons for hope. The Blue Devils squandered a late lead in their 28-24 loss to North Carolina, their first home defeat of the season, and the result ended their own ACC title hopes. But there were bright spots: freshman quarterback Grayson Loftis threw for a career-high 278 yards, and wide receiver Cooper Barkate caught a career-high eight passes for 105 yards. Duke has also been strong in November, winning nine of its last 11 games in the month — though this one, on the road against a top-10 team, is a different kind of test.

The people paid to pick the game are not expecting a surprise. All three predictors at the Fayetteville Observer pick Wake Forest to win, by scores of 38-20, 31-24 and 34-27, and all six of The Chronicle’s beat writers pick the Demon Deacons too, with predicted margins ranging from a touchdown to three touchdowns. The consensus is that Duke can keep it close for a while — then Wake Forest’s offence takes over.

Why it matters

For Wake Forest, Saturday is about turning a surprise season into a championship season. A win books a trip to the ACC title game, almost certainly against Miami, the one team that has beaten them — a rematch with everything on the line. It would also cap one of the best seasons in program history, in front of a national television audience and a home crowd that has waited years for a day like this.

For the ACC, the game settles a title race that has been tightening for weeks. Virginia sits atop the standings at 10-1 and 7-0 in the league, and Miami survived an overtime scare against Pitt, 27-24, to keep its own playoff hopes alive. Wake Forest is the team with the most to gain on Saturday — and the one whose defeat would throw the race open again. Louisville’s 41-0 demolition of Kentucky showed how quickly a statement win can reshape the conversation; Wake Forest has the same chance, at home, in the spotlight.

And for Duke, there is the oldest prize in sport: the spoiler’s role. A win in Winston-Salem would not rescue the Blue Devils’ season, but it would end Wake Forest’s title dream on the biggest stage the Demon Deacons have played on all year — and give a rivalry game a result people would be talking about long after the GameDay trucks have left town.
