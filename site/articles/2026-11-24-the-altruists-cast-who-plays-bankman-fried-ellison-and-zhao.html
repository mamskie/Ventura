The Altruists Cast: Who Plays Bankman-Fried, Ellison and Zhao — and Why Crypto Is Furious

Netflix's The Altruists arrived on November 19 dramatising one of the most notorious financial collapses in American history — and within days, the people who lived it were pushing back. Anthony Boyle plays Sam Bankman-Fried, Julia Garner plays Caroline Ellison and Terry Chen plays Binance co-founder Changpeng Zhao in the eight-part drama about the rise and fall of the FTX cryptocurrency exchange. But the loudest reaction has not come from television critics. It has come from the crypto industry itself, which says the show gets the story wrong — and blames the wrong man.

The backlash has been swift. Zhao, known across crypto simply as CZ, called the series "insane" on X and questioned whether it was designed to shape public opinion. Ethereum co-founder Vitalik Buterin, Binance co-founder Yi He, OKX founder Star Xu and former Binance executive Tigran Gambaryan have all weighed in, accusing the drama of softening the guilt of Bankman-Fried and Ellison while turning Zhao into a villain. Reviewers, meanwhile, have praised the two leads: The Guardian gave the series four stars, calling Boyle and Garner "two of the best young screen actors working today."

What happened

The Altruists dramatises the brief, vertiginous career of Bankman-Fried, who, with Ellison, built FTX into an exchange worth $36 billion at its peak before it collapsed in November 2022. The series opens in 2016, with the pair meeting at the trading firm where Bankman-Fried worked, bonding over maths, their Ivy League professor parents, veganism and a shared belief in effective altruism — the idea that the best thing to do is make as much money as possible, then give it all away. Bankman-Fried founded his first company, Alameda Research, in 2017, and Ellison later joined him there.

The story then moves between the rise in New York in 2016, a precarious peak in Hong Kong in 2018 and the fall in the Bahamas in 2022. At the heart of the collapse was an $8 billion hole in the finances of Alameda and FTX, where customer deposits had been mixed with trading funds — money that should never have been touched. FTX filed for bankruptcy in November 2022 after a liquidity crisis exposed the depth of the problem; investigators later found billions of dollars in customer funds had been diverted to Alameda Research. In March 2024, Bankman-Fried was sentenced to 25 years in prison after being convicted of fraud and conspiracy, and was ordered to forfeit $11 billion. Ellison pleaded guilty to fraud and conspiracy, testified against him and was sentenced to two years. Bankman-Fried maintains he is innocent and is seeking Supreme Court review of his conviction.

Three very different performances sit at the centre of the story. Boyle plays Bankman-Fried as a man convinced his own models could perfect the world; Garner plays Ellison as his on-off partner and closest collaborator; Chen plays Zhao, the rival exchange founder whose decision to sell Binance's holdings of FTX's FTT token intensified the pressure on FTX in its final days. Karan Soni plays one of the most badly hurt of Bankman-Fried's circle. Graham Moore, the show's creator and co-showrunner, has said the series is not really about finance or crypto at all. "The story we're telling is not a financial story, and it's not a crypto story. It's a deeply personal story," he told Business Insider, adding that he drew on extensive research into the real figures' backgrounds and public statements to imagine what drove them apart.

A deeper look: the backlash, and who is saying what

The sharpest criticism has come from Zhao himself and those around him. Zhao pleaded guilty in 2023 to failing to maintain an effective anti-money laundering programme at Binance, in a case separate from the FTX fraud, and was sentenced to four months in prison in 2024 before being pardoned by President Donald Trump in October 2025. In the series, his role in FTX's final days is presented as decisive: shortly before the collapse, Zhao announced Binance would liquidate its remaining FTT holdings. Several crypto figures argue the drama inflates that moment into something it was not.

Crypto commentator Kaleo wrote on X that the show portrays Bankman-Fried and Ellison as having been pressured and groomed by Zhao into their crimes, adding: "Whoever wrote this script is shameless." Buterin said in a reply on X that the show exaggerated Zhao's accent to the point of raising questions about whether the portrayal was racist. Gambaryan wrote on LinkedIn that the series downplayed Bankman-Fried's responsibility while portraying Zhao as a "cartoonish villain," a choice he said undermined the showrunners' credibility. Yi He called the characters "one-dimensional" and "stereotyped," said the show shifted blame away from those responsible, and compared it unfavourably with the Chinese short-form drama platform Hongguo — saying Netflix "might as well be canceled." Star Xu, meanwhile, said Zhao should answer the criticism with facts and evidence rather than dismiss it as coordinated attacks by competitors.

The dispute also has a legal shadow: Netflix has previously faced defamation lawsuits over dramatisations of real events, including Inventing Anna and Baby Reindeer. Rachel DeLoache Williams, a former Vanity Fair photo editor, sued Netflix in 2022 over her portrayal in Inventing Anna, a series about convicted fraudster Anna Sorokin. So far the criticism has played out almost entirely on social media — but the stakes of getting a recent, real-life story wrong are familiar territory for the streamer.

Why it matters

The argument over The Altruists is about more than one television show: who gets to tell the story of an $8 billion fraud while its central figure is still in prison and appealing his conviction — and how quickly a dramatisation can harden into the version of events people remember. The Guardian's review suggests the series itself is worth watching, calling it a high-wire act that "stays just the right side of coherent" and warning viewers not to lose focus for a moment. But the fury from crypto's biggest names shows how much is at stake when the audience includes the people being portrayed.

For viewers, the takeaway is simple: watch it as a drama, not a documentary. The show's own creator says he cannot prove what was going on in the hearts or minds of Bankman-Fried's friends when they made their decisions — and the court record — a 25-year sentence, an $11 billion forfeiture, two years for a cooperating witness — tells a plainer story than any script. The Altruists may be the most talked-about dramatisation of the crypto era, but the argument it started is a reminder that the real story of FTX is still being contested, in courtrooms and on timelines, four years after the exchange collapsed.
