Justin Timberlake Faces His Past in Apple TV Docuseries 'Timberlake'

Apple TV released the first trailer on Monday for Timberlake, a four-part docuseries about Justin Timberlake that the streamer bills as a "raw, reflective and revealing" look at three decades in the spotlight. Directed by Academy Award winner Fisher Stevens, the series premieres on December 11, with the first two episodes arriving together, the third following on December 18 and the finale on December 23.

The trailer moves quickly between home videos of a young Timberlake, footage from his years in 'N Sync and clips from his solo career — and then into the moments that have defined his public image, for better and worse. "I'm sure you're going to go back and see these incredibly sweet and goofy things from my childhood and some things that are really hard to talk about," Timberlake says in the trailer. "For the first time in my life I'm coming to a place to say I can't handle it all."

What the trailer shows

Across its four episodes, Timberlake promises exclusive interviews and previously unseen archival footage spanning roughly three decades of music and popular culture, from Timberlake's earliest television appearances to the present day. Before he became a global pop star, he appeared on Star Search at the age of 11, and the series traces his rise from a member of the boy band 'N Sync to solo stardom.

The people around him get a say too. His wife, Jessica Biel, appears in the trailer, as do his mother, Lynn Harless, and longtime collaborators Pharrell Williams and Timbaland, along with Saturday Night Live creator Lorne Michaels. Biel, whom Timberlake calls "the best thing that ever happened to me," says in the trailer: "I think we've got to address some things."

The trailer also flashes the headlines that have followed Timberlake for years, including descriptions of him as "an opportunistic ball of slime" and "enormously degrading to women," alongside a prediction that he "will never make a comeback." It is an unusually blunt framing for a celebrity documentary — one that suggests the series intends to confront the criticism rather than glide past it.

The moments Timberlake is revisiting

The trailer returns to his relationship with Britney Spears, whom he met as a pre-teen on The All-New Mickey Mouse Club before the two rose to pop music royalty together in the 1990s. The pair drew renewed public scrutiny in 2023 after Spears's memoir, The Woman in Me, revisited their time together and how Timberlake publicly handled their break-up. In the memoir, Spears described hearing rumours that he had cheated with several women, including "various dancers and groupies" and another unnamed celebrity. "We were on the road together and we were all seeing the world," Timberlake says in the trailer. His mother adds: "The level of craziness when those two were together was the definition of insanity."

It also revisits the 2004 Super Bowl halftime show, when Timberlake tore off a piece of Janet Jackson's costume at the end of their duet, exposing her on live television in an incident he famously called a "wardrobe malfunction." Jackson faced severe industry backlash and public scrutiny in the aftermath, while Timberlake's career continued largely unscathed at the time. He apologised in 2021, writing on social media: "I am deeply sorry for the times in my life where my actions contributed to the problem.... I fell short in these moments and in many others and benefited from a system that condones misogyny and racism." The trailer pairs that fallout with footage of a teenage Timberlake showing off a Janet Jackson poster in his bedroom.

The series also touches on his June 2024 arrest for driving while intoxicated in Sag Harbor, New York, for which he later pleaded guilty to a reduced charge of driving while impaired. The arrest became an internet meme after footage showed Timberlake telling the officer, "This is going to ruin the tour," referring to his Forget Tomorrow World Tour, only for the officer, who did not recognise him, to reply: "What tour?" A judge sentenced him to a $500 fine with a $260 surcharge and 25 hours of community service.

Why the series is a gamble

Reaction to the trailer has so far been mixed. "Literally who asked for a Justin Timberlake documentary," one user wrote on X, while another added: "I don't think Justin Timberlake making a documentary about himself is going to get the public reaction he's hoping for." The scepticism is part of the story the series is telling: a star who has spent years as a punchline asking an audience to take him seriously on his own terms.

Stevens is a canny choice of director for that task. The Academy Award winner previously worked on The Cove and the 2023 Netflix docuseries Beckham, so he arrives with a record of letting famous subjects tell their own stories at length. Timberlake is produced by Stevens alongside Maura Anderson, with Blackbird Presents Films, Misher Films and Highly Flammable behind the project — a team built for glossy, archive-rich celebrity storytelling.

Whether it works will depend on how much Timberlake is willing to say. A documentary that dwells only on the hits would be easy to dismiss; one that gives real space to the Spears years, the Super Bowl fallout and the arrest — and to Biel's frank presence in the trailer — could land differently. The staggered release, with two episodes on December 11 and the rest across the following twelve days, gives the conversation time to build — and time for the verdict to harden. For a singer whose career has been defined as much by its controversies as its choruses, the series is less a victory lap than a test: whether three decades of fame can be retold by the person who lived them, flaws and all.
