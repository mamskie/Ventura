Injured Alcaraz Reaches ATP Finals Semifinals — and a Rematch With Shelton

Carlos Alcaraz is one win away from his first ATP Finals title match — and he got there on a sore left thigh, a taped right knee, and a game plan built around hitting the ball as hard as he could and moving as little as possible. The world No. 2 beat home favourite Flavio Cobolli 6-4, 6-1 in Turin on Friday to complete a perfect group stage at the Nitto ATP Finals, setting up a semifinal rematch with Ben Shelton on Saturday.

The semifinal lineup is the strongest the season-ending championship can offer: the top four seeds all advanced. Top-ranked Alexander Zverev will face Daniil Medvedev in the other semifinal, while Shelton — the 24-year-old American chasing history — gets another shot at the man who beat him in the Paris Masters final twelve days ago. For Alcaraz, though, the bigger question is not who stands across the net. It is whether his body will hold up for two more matches.

What happened

Alcaraz arrived in Turin carrying questions about his fitness. He hurt his left thigh in his previous group match against Medvedev, and he took the court against Cobolli with an elastic black band on the injured leg and a strip of tape on his right knee. The injury is the latest in a season disrupted by physical problems: a right wrist injury kept him out for four and a half months this year, forcing him to miss the French Open and Wimbledon.

On Friday, none of it showed. Alcaraz broke Cobolli's serve in the opening game of both sets and finished points quickly, piling up a 29-9 advantage in winners. “I tried to play as aggressive as I can — hitting the ball as hard as I could, trying not to move that much,” Alcaraz said afterwards. The 6-4, 6-1 win was his third in three group matches, and it stretched his winning streak to 12 matches after titles in Vienna and Paris.

The other semifinalists earned their places with less drama. Medvedev beat Frances Tiafoe 6-3, 6-4 to set up his meeting with Zverev, the top seed. Shelton, meanwhile, booked his semifinal spot earlier in the week and will play in the first semifinal on Saturday, scheduled to start no earlier than 2:30 p.m. local time in Turin.

A deeper look: a rematch with unfinished business

The Alcaraz–Shelton semifinal is a rematch with a short but charged history. The two last met at the U.S. Open, where Shelton won a five-set quarterfinal that did not finish until 3:33 a.m. Alcaraz answered twelve days ago in the Paris Masters final, beating Shelton to claim the title and extend his winning run. Alcaraz leads their head-to-head series 4-1.

Shelton has more than revenge on his mind. The 24-year-old is playing the season finale for only the second time in his career, and he is trying to become the first American to win the title since Pete Sampras in 1999. A U.S. Open finalist, Shelton has made the leap from crowd favourite to genuine contender — and a win over Alcaraz in Turin would be the biggest of his career.

Alcaraz, for his part, is chasing a first. He has won seven Grand Slam titles, but the ATP Finals trophy still eludes him: he lost to Jannik Sinner in last year's championship match. Sinner, who ended his season early with a right knee injury, is not in Turin to defend his title. Medvedev, the 2020 champion, is the man Alcaraz hurt his thigh against — and he now stands in Zverev's path in the other semifinal.

The other semifinal has its own history. Medvedev, the 2020 champion, reached the last four by beating Frances Tiafoe 6-3, 6-4, and the Russian's win also carried a strange footnote: Tiafoe was hit with a time violation and lost his first serve on the very point where Medvedev broke for a 5-4 lead in the second set. Against Zverev, the top-ranked player in the world, Medvedev will need more than a break of fortune — he will need to beat the man who has led the rankings into the season finale. The winner faces either Alcaraz or Shelton in the final, with the last trophy of the year on the line.

Why it matters

First, the semifinals decide whether the season ends with a coronation or a surprise. Alcaraz has won twelve matches in a row and beaten everyone put in front of him since Paris, but he is doing it on a thigh he injured earlier in the week. “The challenge now is not on the court; it's off the court,” he said on Friday, describing the hours with his physio and doctor as “a really important half a day.” If the injury holds, he is the favourite. If it doesn't, the draw opens up fast.

Second, the tournament is a test of the next generation's staying power. Alcaraz, Shelton, Zverev and Medvedev are the top four seeds, and all four are in the semifinals — a rare alignment at the season finale. Shelton's rise in particular gives American tennis a storyline it has waited for since Sampras: a home-grown contender at the very top of the game, one win from a final.

Third, Saturday's matches set up a final that could define the year. An Alcaraz–Zverev final would pit the world's top two against each other for the season's last trophy; a Shelton upset would put a first-time champion one match from history. Either way, Turin has delivered the ending the season deserved: the four best players of the year, in the semifinals, with everything still to play for.

The semifinals begin Saturday afternoon in Turin. Alcaraz and Shelton open the day; Zverev and Medvedev follow. Two matches, four players, one trophy — and for the injured favourite, a race against his own body that began the moment he walked off the court on Friday.
