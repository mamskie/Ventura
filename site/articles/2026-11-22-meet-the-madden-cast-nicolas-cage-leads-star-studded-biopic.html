Meet the 'Madden' Cast: Nicolas Cage Leads a Star-Studded Biopic With a Divided Verdict

Searches for "madden cast" surged across the United States this week as Amazon's new biopic "Madden" arrived on Prime Video — and the first question most viewers are asking is a simple one: who is playing whom? The answer is a cast list that reads like an awards-season roll call. Nicolas Cage plays John Madden, Christian Bale plays longtime Raiders owner Al Davis, Kathryn Hahn plays Madden's wife Virginia, and John Mulaney plays Trip Hawkins, the video-game pioneer who put Madden's name on the best-selling football game in history.

Directed by David O. Russell, the film traces Madden from the Raiders sideline to the broadcast booth and beyond, and it debuted on Prime Video on Wednesday, just in time for Thanksgiving week. It runs 110 minutes and is rated R for language throughout. But while the star power has pushed the film to the top of the search charts, the early critical verdict is far less celebratory: reviewers say the movie, for all its famous faces, cannot settle on a game plan.

What happened

The film opens with Madden hellbent on bringing a Super Bowl title to the Raiders, follows him through a coaching career cut short by a bleeding ulcer, and tracks his reinvention as a broadcaster — the plain-spoken, telestrator-wielding voice who explained the game to a generation of Sunday viewers. It ends, abruptly, with Al Davis's induction into the Pro Football Hall of Fame. Along the way it touches on the third act that made Madden a household name even for people who never watched a down of football: the video game that carries his name.

The cast is the film's clearest selling point. Cage, an Oscar winner for "Leaving Las Vegas", underwent a major physical transformation for the role — leaked photos from the shoot showed him heavier-set, in prosthetics, chasing Madden's unmistakable bulk. Bale, a four-time Oscar nominee and winner for "The Fighter", takes on Davis, one of the most polarising figures in NFL history. Hahn plays Virginia Fields Madden; Mulaney plays Hawkins, who led Electronic Arts in making Madden the face of its football game; Sienna Miller plays Davis's wife Carol Sagal Davis; Joel Murray plays legendary broadcaster Pat Summerall; Shane Gillis, in his first film role, plays Madden's neighbour Vince McGary; and former Raider Miles Burris — 38 games for Oakland from 2012 to 2014 — plays former linebacker Phil Villapiano. Snoop Dogg and Marshawn Lynch turn up in cameos.

Amazon MGM gave the film a straight-to-streaming release on Prime Video, a deliberate piece of scheduling: a football film landing the week of Thanksgiving, when the sport dominates the American living room. The timing is part of the story, and the search numbers suggest plenty of viewers took the invitation.

A deeper look: a star cast, a film searching for a playbook

The Associated Press review, published in the Boston Herald, gave the film one and a half stars out of four, and its complaint is not a lack of effort. The review finds the film's affection for its subject palpable — Madden the Super Bowl-winning coach, broadcasting legend and video game icon — but argues that it "never settles on an approach, a tone or a focus", with performances swinging between broad parody and earnest drama. Cage's prosthetic-aided turn, it says, feels like a well-meaning misfire rather than a real travesty: a goofy comedy that can't find the end zone, let alone the 50-yard line. Above all, the voice never stops feeling like Nicolas Cage doing John Madden.

Part of the trouble is that Russell, who wrote the script with Cambron Clark, is trying to make three films at once. For about twenty minutes it is a story about family and football, and a marriage perpetually on the brink. Sometimes it is a story about Madden's enduring friendship with Davis, the slick-haired, Machiavellian owner. Occasionally it is a story about a coach learning to accept life after football. The film's best stretch, the review says, comes after the retirement, when Madden finds himself explaining 3-4 defences to his dog. Hahn, as Virginia, is the most reliable source of coherence — the film briefly comes together whenever she is talking. Mulaney gets one of the few outright laughs, with a riff on the barbershop scene from "Chinatown" late in the film.

Bale brings a hard edge to Davis that finds little to play against, while Miller's Carol runs as a kind of mirror to the Maddens' marriage — Virginia refuses to yield their lives entirely to football. Gillis's appearance, meanwhile, is judged an awkward and pointless insertion. Russell, the director of "Silver Linings Playbook" and "American Hustle", has always favoured a loose, screwball zaniness that can lift off or fall flat, the review notes. Here, it argues, the improvisational energy produces plenty of big personalities, wigs and prosthetics — and very little that feels sincere and wholehearted.

Why it matters

The search surge is easy to understand, because Madden himself is a three-career American icon: a Super Bowl-winning coach, a broadcasting legend, and a video-game namesake whose game introduced millions of young fans to the sport. A biopic about him was always going to draw a crowd — and a cast this famous guaranteed that the first question would be about the casting, not the reviews. People want to see Cage as Madden, Bale as Davis, and a comedian-heavy supporting cast take on figures many viewers remember from real life.

The portrayal of Davis is already a live argument beyond the reviews. Raiders owner Mark Davis has said publicly that he is unhappy with the film's depiction of his father — a reaction that turns a streaming biopic into a family-and-legacy dispute playing out in public. It is the kind of response that keeps a film in the conversation long after its opening weekend, whatever the star ratings say.

And the timing is no accident. A football film arriving on a streaming service in Thanksgiving week, when the NFL owns the American weekend, is built to be watched between games — and argued about at the table. Whether viewers side with the critics or with the cast's star power will decide whether "Madden" becomes a holiday curiosity or a lasting entry in the sports-biopic canon.

One thing is certain: a film that cannot pick a play has still managed to pick a fight with the search charts — and win it.
