The Hunger Games Roars Back: Sunrise on the Reaping Opens to $120 Million

Fourteen years and six films in, The Hunger Games has found its box-office power again. “The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping” opened with $120 million in North American theatres over its first weekend, according to studio estimates reported on Sunday, and $225 million worldwide — a debut that restores Suzanne Collins’s franchise to the blockbuster tier it seemed to have left behind.

The result is all the more striking for the competition it faced. The film opened the same weekend Grand Theft Auto VI arrived, a release many expected to keep would-be moviegoers at home. Instead, theatres filled — largely with young audiences — and the weekend became one of the year’s biggest.

But the celebration comes with a caveat that Lionsgate itself is making plain: there will be no quick sequel. The studio says the next Hunger Games film depends entirely on Collins writing another book, and the author has given no commitment. For now, the franchise’s future is a waiting game.

What happened

Sunrise on the Reaping, directed by Francis Lawrence, is a prequel set 25 years before the original series, centred on a teenage Haymitch Abernathy, the character Woody Harrelson played in the earlier films. The young Haymitch is played by Jo Zada, leading a new generation of actors — Mckenna Grace, Ben Wang and Whitney Peak — alongside veteran stars including Ralph Fiennes, Kieran Culkin, Elle Fanning and Jesse Plemons.

The opening dwarfs the franchise’s last outing. The 2023 prequel, The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes, opened to $44 million in North America and finished its run at $361.8 million worldwide — a respectable total, but roughly half of what each of the first four Hunger Games films earned. Sunrise on the Reaping nearly tripled that opening in a single weekend.

It is also the year’s fifth domestic opening above $100 million — a club that in 2026 has included only the most bankable names: Spider-Man: Brand New Day, Toy Story 5, The Super Mario Galaxy Movie and Christopher Nolan’s The Odyssey. It is the highest-grossing Hunger Games film since 2014’s Mockingjay: Part One. Reviews were solid rather than ecstatic — 67% fresh on Rotten Tomatoes — but audiences liked it more: an A- CinemaScore, up from the B+ earned by Songbirds and Snakes.

The audience skewed young and female. Some 74% of ticket buyers were aged 18 to 34, and 67% were women, according to figures cited by the Associated Press — the latest sign of a year in which Gen Z and Gen Alpha have powered one theatrical hit after another. The previews had already hinted at the scale: the film took $21 million in Thursday previews, the second-best preview result of 2026.

A deeper look: a comeback built on the right story

Lionsgate’s motion picture chief, Adam Fogelson, credits the turnaround to the story itself. Songbirds and Snakes was an origin story for a villain — President Coriolanus Snow — and a darker, less bombastic tale. Sunrise on the Reaping puts the Games themselves back at the centre and tells the backstory of a character audiences already loved and wanted to know more about: Haymitch. That choice, the studio believes, is what relit the fire — and it is why Lionsgate invested in the production and the casting the way it did.

The return of familiar faces helped too. Jennifer Lawrence and Josh Hutcherson are back, and the film’s social-media reach hit 652 million, according to RelishMix — a measure of how much of the conversation around the opening happened online, among the same young audiences who filled the seats.

The timing gamble also paid off. Lionsgate weighed the risks of opening against Grand Theft Auto VI from the earliest stages of dating the film, and concluded the two could coexist. Paul Dergarabedian of Rentrak put it bluntly: the marketplace has spoken, and both can do just fine. The weekend’s other numbers backed him up. Amazon MGM’s I Play Rocky took second place with $9.3 million — and a rare A+ CinemaScore — while last week’s No. 1, Ebenezer, fell 60% to $7.7 million.

Yet the same studio that is celebrating is also urging patience. Fogelson told The Hollywood Reporter that Lionsgate has complete respect for Suzanne Collins as the architect of the franchise, and that he told her he would drop everything and travel anywhere in the world to read her next book — and then sit patiently and wait for her call. Collins herself, asked about another novel, answered only: “We’ll see.”

Why it matters

For Lionsgate, the opening changes the arithmetic of a franchise that had looked diminished. A $150 million production that opens to $225 million worldwide has room to be very profitable, and it proves the series can still draw a crowd without its original star at the centre. Jennifer Lawrence was not the mega movie star she is now when the franchise started with her, as Fogelson noted; this time, a mix of newcomers and veterans carried the film.

For the wider industry, the weekend is another data point in the year’s defining story: young audiences are going to the movies, and they are choosing event films. The same demographic powered several of 2026’s hits, and Sunrise on the Reaping’s 74% under-35 audience is the clearest version of the pattern yet. It also shows that a video-game launch and a blockbuster opening can share a weekend — a question Hollywood will keep asking as games grow into ever-larger cultural events.

For fans, the wait begins. The books’ scope now runs from the 50th Hunger Games to the 74th, and the fan base has made clear which stories it wants next — Finnick Odair’s and Johanna Mason’s among them. Whether any of them sparks Collins to write again is, as Fogelson put it, a matter of waiting for a phone call. Until then, the odds of another Hunger Games film soon look long — but after this weekend, nobody at Lionsgate seems in a hurry to force the issue. The franchise has its fire back; the question is what Collins chooses to do with it.
