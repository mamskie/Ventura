Ella Langley Chases History at the 2026 CMA Awards

Ella Langley arrived at the 60th CMA Awards on Wednesday night as the most-nominated artist in country music — and she was already a winner before the show began. The Alabama singer leads the field with nine nominations, including her first in the night’s top category, entertainer of the year, at a ceremony marking six decades of the Country Music Association Awards.

Two of her prizes were decided before the ceremony even started. Her blockbuster single “Choosin’ Texas” won single of the year and music video of the year, with the early results announced on ABC’s Good Morning America on Wednesday morning. A third early award, musical event of the year, went to “McArthur,” the collaboration from HARDY, Eric Church, Morgan Wallen and Tim McGraw.

The show itself takes place at Bridgestone Arena in Nashville, hosted by Lainey Wilson, with Taylor Swift performing on the CMA stage for the first time in 13 years. It airs Wednesday at 8 p.m. EST and 7 p.m. CST on ABC and Disney+, and a replay will be available from Thursday on Hulu. Langley is among the night’s performers, alongside Swift, Kacey Musgraves, Luke Combs and Megan Moroney.

What happened

The early wins set the tone for a night that could end with Langley making CMA history. “Choosin’ Texas” took single of the year — an award that goes to the artist, producers and mix engineer — with production by Miranda Lambert, Langley and Ben West and mixing by Dave Clauss. The song beat a field that included Langley’s own follow-up single “Be Her,” Taylor Swift’s “I Knew It, I Knew You,” Lainey Wilson’s “Somewhere Over Laredo” and Cody Johnson’s “The Fall.”

The music video prize went to the same song, directed by Langley herself with Caylee Robillard and Wales Toney. The two early wins mean Langley was already the night’s most decorated artist before the televised ceremony began — and the five biggest prizes were still to come.

Those five are the categories that define a CMA night: entertainer of the year, album of the year, single of the year, song of the year and female vocalist of the year. Langley is nominated in all five. No woman has ever won all five in a single night; the last artist to manage a sweep of that scale was Alan Jackson in 2002, when he took entertainer, single, song and album honours along with male vocalist of the year.

A closer look at the record on the line

The engine of Langley’s night is a single song. “Choosin’ Texas,” a twangy ballad about longing and learning to let go, spent 28 non-consecutive weeks at No. 1 on the all-genre Billboard Hot 100 — a record-setting run for any song in the chart’s history. Langley co-wrote it with Luke Dick, Miranda Lambert and Joybeth Taylor, and its crossover success has carried it into both the single of the year and song of the year races.

She is, remarkably, competing against herself in both categories: her radio follow-up “Be Her” is also nominated for single and song of the year. The song of the year field is rounded out by “Beautiful Things,” “Dry Spell” and “Gary,” while the single race also features Swift, Wilson and Johnson. A year ago, Langley won in both categories with her Riley Green duet “You Look Like You Love Me,” so a repeat is far from unthinkable.

The album race may be her toughest test. Her breakout album Dandelion is up against Zach Top’s Ain’t In It For My Health, which won the Grammy for best traditional country album earlier this year; Parker McCollum’s self-titled album, which took album of the year at the ACM Awards last spring; Kacey Musgraves’ Middle of Nowhere, which powered four Grammy nominations for Musgraves earlier this week; and Luke Combs’ The Way I Am.

In female vocalist of the year, Langley faces Musgraves, Lambert — who co-wrote “Choosin’ Texas” and released a new album, Crisco, last month — Megan Moroney, who is on an arena tour behind her third album Cloud 9, and Wilson, a two-time entertainer of the year winner. And the entertainer race itself is a heavyweight field: Johnson, who won entertainer of the year at the rival ACM Awards in May; Wilson, who won the CMA’s top prize in 2023 and 2025; Combs, who won it in 2021 and 2022; and Chris Stapleton, a five-time nominee for the award who has yet to win it.

Why it matters

If Langley completes the sweep, she will be the first woman to win all five of the CMA Awards’ major categories in one night — a milestone at the show’s 60th anniversary, and a marker of how completely country music’s centre of gravity has shifted in a single year. Her rise has been fast: a small-town Alabama native who stormed the Billboard charts this year, she now leads the night’s nominations and already owns two of its prizes.

The night also matters because of what it says about country music’s place in the mainstream. A country song holding the Hot 100’s top spot for 28 weeks — longer than any song in Billboard history — is not a genre story but a pop-chart story, and the CMA stage reflected it: Swift’s first performance on the show in 13 years, a 60th-anniversary bill, and a field of nominees that spans country’s traditional wing and its crossover stars.

There is a competitive edge to the evening as well. A Langley sweep would deny Stapleton yet another shot at the entertainer prize he has chased for years, and it would end Wilson’s run as the show’s dominant force of the past two years. Whatever happens, the two early wins already guarantee that Langley leaves Nashville with more CMA Awards than she arrived with — and the night’s biggest prizes will decide whether she leaves with a piece of history as well.
