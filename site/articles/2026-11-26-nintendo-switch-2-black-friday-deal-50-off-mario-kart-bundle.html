Nintendo Switch 2 Black Friday Deal Takes $50 Off the Mario Kart Bundle

Nintendo has announced its first official discount on the Switch 2, and it is the deal holiday shoppers have been waiting for. The Mario Kart World bundle, which normally sells for $549.99, drops to $499.99 in Nintendo’s Black Friday sale, which began on November 22 and runs through November 29 — with the bundle offer extended through December 5. The announcement turned “switch 2 black friday” into one of the most searched phrases in the United States.

The cut arrives less than three months after Nintendo raised the price of the standalone Switch 2 in the United States, from $449.99 to $499.99 in September. In cash terms, the $50 discount exactly wipes out that increase — but only for buyers who take the bundle, which at $499.99 still costs $50 more than the standalone console did before September.

Buyers get a console, a copy of Mario Kart World and three months of Nintendo Switch Online membership. Nintendo puts the total saving at about $88 against buying the same items separately. The offer is available at Amazon, Best Buy, Walmart, Target, GameStop and the Nintendo Store, while supplies last.

What happened

Nintendo confirmed the sale in its Black Friday announcement, headlined by the $50 discount on the Switch 2’s Mario Kart World bundle. The wider sale runs from November 22 through November 29, but the bundle offer extends through December 5. Black Friday itself falls on November 27 this year, with Cyber Monday following on November 30.

Alongside the console deal, Nintendo is offering $20 off selected Switch 2 games through November 29: Donkey Kong Bananza, Metroid Prime 4: Beyond – Nintendo Switch 2 Edition, Kirby AirRiders, Super Mario Galaxy 1+2 and Super Mario Party Jamboree, with prices starting at $39.99. Donkey Kong Bananza is the exception: its current US price is $69.99, so it is more likely to sell for $49.99.

This is the first official discount the Switch 2 has received since the console launched in June 2025. A similar bundle was discounted during Black Friday last year, but that deal was led by retailers such as Walmart and Amazon rather than Nintendo itself, took the bundle down to $449.99, and did not include the online membership.

The retailers are already in full swing: Walmart’s Black Friday sale has started, Best Buy’s early sale went live last week, and Amazon’s sale began on November 19. ZDNET reports that the Switch 2 bundle is already one of its readers’ favourite early deals — and notes that Nintendo discounts are rare, with the holiday season one of the few times of year they appear at all.

The deal in detail

The headline numbers are straightforward, but the value depends on how the bundle is counted. The $50 cut is roughly a 9.1% discount on the bundle’s regular $549.99 price, according to TechRepublic. Nintendo says the bundle already saves buyers $37.98 against buying the items separately, thanks to the included membership — so with the discount applied, the total saving is about $88, or nearly 15% for shoppers who already planned to buy everything in the box.

Inside the box are the console, two Joy-Con 2 controllers, a Mario Kart World voucher and three months of Nintendo Switch Online, covering multiplayer play, classic-games libraries and member discounts. A single-profile membership costs $19.99 a year after the included period ends; a family plan covering up to eight profiles starts at $34.99 a year.

The game itself carries real value. The digital edition of Mario Kart World lists for $80 on its own, so as ZDNET puts it, shoppers are not really saving cash on the console itself — but they are getting a game they would probably buy anyway at a lower effective price. For first-time buyers, that is the calculation that matters: console and game in one purchase, with no separate game to buy.

The hardware is a step up from the original Switch: a 7.9-inch LCD with a 120Hz refresh rate, 256GB of storage, and support for microSD Express cards. It also plays compatible games from the original Switch, although some have compatibility limitations, and its 4K output is limited to 60 frames per second.

One bundle is not part of the deal: the Nintendo Switch Sports Resort bundle, which also normally sells for $549.99, will stay at $529.99. Every offer is while supplies last, and ZDNET warns that stock of the Switch 2 bundle is not likely to last long.

Why it matters

The deal matters first because Nintendo so rarely cuts prices. Unlike other console makers, it almost never discounts its own hardware, and when a deal does appear it tends to be driven by retailers rather than the company. A first official discount on a console that launched only in June 2025 is unusual.

For first-time buyers and for parents shopping for holiday gifts, the bundle is the simplest route in: a console and a game without a separate game purchase, and three months of online membership covering multiplayer and classic-games libraries from day one. For that group, the question is less the size of the discount than whether the box has everything needed to start playing.

For existing Switch owners, the decision is more complicated. The larger display, higher resolution and extra storage are real improvements, but the upgrade carries costs beyond the bundle: TechRepublic notes that another $50 on accessories takes the upfront cost to $549.99 before taxes. Its advice is blunt: ask whether you would still buy the Switch 2 without the discount. If yes, the bundle gets you more for your money; if the discount is the main motivation, waiting may be better.

There is also an argument for not waiting. IGN’s commerce team notes that once a Switch deal hits it usually sticks for the rest of the sale — the prices shoppers see now are very likely the ones that will hold through December. There is little reason to expect a deeper cut later, but also little risk in taking a day to compare retailer offers.

The search surge is part of the story. “Switch 2 black friday” became one of the most searched phrases in the United States as the sale went live, a sign of how many shoppers are doing the same calculation: is $50 off a console that went up $50 in September a deal, or just a return to where things were? On the numbers, the answer is both — the discount cancels the price rise, the bundle adds a game and a membership, and whether that is enough depends on what is already under the television.
