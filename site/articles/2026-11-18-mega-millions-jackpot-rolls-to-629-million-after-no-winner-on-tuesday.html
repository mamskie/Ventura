Mega Millions Jackpot Rolls to $629 Million After No Winner on Tuesday

The Mega Millions jackpot is still up for grabs — and it just got a lot bigger. No ticket matched all six numbers in Tuesday night's drawing, sending the top prize rolling over to an estimated $629 million for Friday's drawing, with a cash option of about $258.8 million.

The winning numbers for Tuesday, November 17, were 3, 10, 19, 28 and 33, with a Mega Ball of 21. The drawing, held at 11 p.m. ET, carried an estimated jackpot of $600 million and a cash option of $246.9 million before taxes. It was the second consecutive drawing without a jackpot winner, after Friday's numbers — 10, 22, 33, 49 and 57, with Mega Ball 18 — also failed to produce a match for all six.

Nobody won the jackpot, but the night was not a total loss for players. Two tickets sold in Florida matched all five white balls, missing only the Mega Ball, and each carried a 2X multiplier, making each ticket worth $2 million. It is the kind of near-miss that keeps the game in the headlines — and the reason "mega millions drawing november 17" became one of the most-searched phrases in the United States on Tuesday night.

What happened on Tuesday

Tuesday's drawing was the latest step in a jackpot run that has been building for days. Before Friday's drawing, the jackpot stood at an estimated $555 million. When no ticket matched all six numbers on Friday, the prize climbed to $600 million for Tuesday. Tuesday's result pushed it higher still, to $629 million — the amount now advertised for Friday, November 20.

Mega Millions holds drawings twice a week, on Tuesdays and Fridays at 11 p.m. ET, and the results are released once each draw has been completed and verified. Players who want a shot at Friday's prize have until their state's ticket sales cut-off to buy in. In Michigan, for example, in-store and online sales end at 10:45 p.m. on the night of the drawing, and cut-off times vary from state to state.

Anyone holding a ticket from Tuesday should check it against the official results. The two Florida tickets show why: matching five white balls without the Mega Ball is the game's second prize, and the built-in multiplier decides how much it is worth. With a 2X multiplier, each Florida ticket is worth $2 million; the same match with a 10X multiplier would be worth $10 million.

How the game works — and what the odds really are

A Mega Millions ticket costs $5. Players pick five different numbers from 1 to 70 and one Mega Ball number from 1 to 24, or let the terminal choose with a Quick Pick. The jackpot goes to anyone who matches all six numbers in a drawing.

Every play now carries a built-in multiplier, randomly assigned, that increases non-jackpot prizes by 2X, 3X, 4X, 5X or 10X. The old Megaplier feature has been retired. The multiplier is what turns a $1 million Match 5 prize into anything from $2 million to $10 million, and it applies down the prize table: matching four white balls plus the Mega Ball pays between $20,000 and $100,000 depending on the multiplier, while matching just the Mega Ball alone is worth $10 to $50.

The odds explain why the jackpot can roll for so long. The chance of matching all five white balls and the Mega Ball is 1 in 290,472,336. That is the hardest prize in the game to win, and it is why jackpots can climb through several drawings before anyone claims them — and why they can climb so high when they do.

Winners get a choice. They can take the advertised jackpot as an annuity — one immediate payment followed by 29 annual payments, a structure the Mega Millions website says "helps protect winners' lifestyle and purchasing power in periods of inflation" — or take the cash option, a one-time lump-sum payment equal to all the cash in the jackpot prize pool. If two or more tickets win the jackpot in the same drawing, the money is shared equally among the winners.

Why it matters

A $629 million jackpot is the kind of number that turns a routine lottery drawing into a national conversation. The searches spike every time the prize crosses a round number, and Tuesday was no exception: with $600 million on the line and no winner since Friday, millions of Americans checked their numbers, their tickets and the next drawing date. By Wednesday morning, the drawing had already become one of the day's most-searched topics nationwide, with players in every participating state checking the same six numbers.

The rollover also shows how the game's design works. Because the odds are so long, the jackpot grows with each drawing that produces no winner, and each increase draws in more players. More players mean more tickets sold, which in turn pushes the advertised jackpot higher — a cycle that only ends when someone, somewhere, matches all six numbers.

For now, the wait continues until Friday. The next drawing is November 20 at 11 p.m. ET, and the prize on offer will be an estimated $629 million, or about $258.8 million in cash. Whether the jackpot is won or rolls again, the numbers will be posted after the draw — and the search spike that follows will say as much about the size of the prize as the result itself.
