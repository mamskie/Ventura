Elon Musk Loses $73.8 Billion in a Day as Tesla and SpaceX Slide in AI Sell-Off

Elon Musk lost $73.8 billion in a single day on Friday, according to Forbes’ real-time billionaires list, as a brutal sell-off on Wall Street wiped out gains across the technology sector and dragged down the two companies behind most of his fortune, Tesla and SpaceX. It was one of the sharpest one-day declines ever recorded for a single fortune — and it did not happen in isolation: the world’s five richest people lost a combined $146 billion in the same session.

The scale of the rout was hard to miss. The S&P 500 closed down 3.7 percent at 6,910.39, a seven-month low, while the Nasdaq fell 5.3 percent and the Dow dropped nearly 2 percent. Chip stocks were hit hardest: Nvidia fell 12.4 percent, Broadcom 11.2 percent, and AMD and Micron each lost at least 12 percent. Behind the selling, investors said, is a growing worry that the enormous sums being poured into artificial intelligence — much of it funded by debt — may not produce meaningful profits.

What happened

Musk’s fortune fell 8.34 percent to $811.7 billion on the Forbes count, which keeps him first on the list by a wide margin. Jeff Bezos, in second place, holds $366.8 billion after losing $13.4 billion. Google co-founders Larry Page and Sergey Brin lost $18.1 billion and $16.6 billion respectively, leaving them at $221.5 billion and $204 billion, each down about 7.5 percent. Mark Zuckerberg took the steepest percentage hit among the top five: his fortune dropped $24.2 billion, or 11.13 percent, to $193.1 billion, with Michael Dell now less than $12 billion behind him in sixth place at $181.8 billion.

Estimates of Musk’s one-day loss varied with the tracker and the time of measurement — other tallies put the drop at $64 billion, with his fortune at just over $809 billion — but every measure agreed on the direction and the scale. After the drop, the five richest people are worth a combined $1.8 trillion by the Forbes figures, and the real-time list updates through the day, so the numbers can still change.

The market damage went beyond the billionaires’ list. The S&P 500 slid into correction territory and the Nasdaq 100 into a bear market as the sell-off gathered pace, with the chipmakers that power the AI boom leading the declines.

A deeper look: the AI trade turns on its builders

The two engines of Musk’s fortune both fell hard on Friday. SpaceX stock dropped to $127, down sharply from its all-time high of $225, while Tesla fell to $345 from its October high of $413. Musk holds an almost 20 percent stake in Tesla and is the biggest SpaceX shareholder with a 40 percent stake, so a bad day for either company is a bad day for his net worth — and Friday was a bad day for both.

The selling was driven by the same fear that has stalked the market for weeks: that heavy AI spending, much of it funded by debt, may not produce meaningful profits. SpaceX is a large player in the AI space because of its ownership of Grok and its data center business, and data center companies including CoreWeave and Nebius have slumped on rising concerns of a slowdown in the sector. The pressure intensified when Anthropic, a top competitor to Grok, plunged in its second day as a publicly traded company, with its valuation falling to $1.18 trillion — a drop that invites investors to reassess what Grok itself is worth.

SpaceX’s own numbers show why the market is nervous. Its Superintelligence division generated more than $7.56 billion in revenue in its latest earnings report, or 52 percent of the total, but it is also the company’s most capital-intensive segment, with capital expenditure rising to more than $16.4 billion. Tesla, meanwhile, is positioning itself as a top player in AI through full self-driving cars, robotaxis and the Optimus humanoid robot, and is a partner with SpaceX on the Terafab project — but there are concerns its growth will remain under pressure, especially in China, where competition has risen and led to a price war.

Friday was not the first time Musk’s fortune has swung by tens of billions in a day. His fortune fell $79.7 billion in a day on November 6, and $34 billion the day before that, after losing $68 billion in October. On October 22, Forbes recorded a $79.2 billion one-day drop, from $989 billion to $909.8 billion, after SpaceX fell 9.14 percent and Tesla 3.08 percent. The fortune peaked at over $1.3 trillion following the SpaceX IPO in June, when Musk became the first person ever confirmed with a net worth above $1 trillion.

The arithmetic explains the whiplash. SpaceX accounts for about 81 percent of Musk’s fortune: Forbes counts about 38 percent of SpaceX, including options, against nearly 11 percent of Tesla. At recent prices, a 1 percent move in SpaceX is worth about $7.6 billion to Musk, including the options, while a 1 percent move in Tesla is worth about $1.5 billion.

Why it matters

Musk’s fortune has become a proxy for the AI trade itself. When confidence in AI spending holds, his net worth climbs by tens of billions in a session; when it wobbles, the world’s richest person can lose more in a day than most companies are worth in total. Friday’s drop was the third huge one-day fall in about six weeks, and each one has been driven by the same question: how much is the AI buildout really worth?

The warnings are getting louder. IMF Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva said, “We have a world economy that is standing on one leg, on the AI leg,” and that a wobble could cause financial stability problems. The sell-off is already reshaping the rest of the rich list: Larry Ellison fell from ninth to twelfth with about $137.7 billion, while Nvidia’s Jensen Huang has dropped to seventeenth, his wealth down to $105 billion from a year-to-date high of nearly $200 billion.

For now, the hierarchy at the top has not changed — Musk’s $811.7 billion is still more than double Bezos in second place. What has changed is the mood. The trillion-dollar milestone Musk reached in June now looks distant, and the market’s verdict on Friday was that the AI boom’s biggest fortunes are only as solid as the spending behind them. With Forbes’ real-time list updating through the day, the next move in the fortune will come with the next move in the trade.
